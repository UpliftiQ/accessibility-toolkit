# Adoption guides

## Developers

Start with one real media flow. Use the caption player without replacing native controls, add a transcript, then run the accessibility test matrix. Adopt other packages only when the product needs them.

## NGOs and community organisations

- Add accurate captions and transcripts to training media.
- Record sign-language consent and rights.
- Test on lower-cost phones and slower connections.
- Provide downloadable text when video access is expensive.
- Invite community members to review before a public programme.

## Schools and universities

- Publish captions before the lesson, not weeks later.
- Make transcripts searchable and downloadable.
- Keep sign-language video visible beside slides or demonstrations.
- Use visual timers and alerts in assessments.
- Provide accessible alternatives for live and recorded content.

## Businesses and product teams

- Use visual alerts for onboarding and internal training.
- Make customer-education videos caption-first.
- Include accessibility states in the design system and QA process.
- Do not collect disability information through the toolkit.
- Assign ownership for caption quality and remediation.

## Government and public services

- Prioritise essential instructions and time-sensitive information.
- Support the sign language and written languages used by the audience.
- Keep files usable on mobile devices and constrained networks.
- Provide a clear contact path when information is inaccessible.

## Procurement questions

Ask vendors:

- Can users control captions, transcript and sign-video layout?
- Does the core flow work by keyboard?
- Are media rights and consent documented?
- What manual testing was completed?
- Which limitations remain?
- Can the organisation export its media and text in standard formats?
