# Sign-language media guidance

## Record the language precisely

Sign languages are distinct languages. Record `Indian Sign Language`, `American Sign Language` or the correct language name instead of saying “sign language” as if it were universal.

## Framing

- Keep hands, face and upper body visible.
- Use stable lighting and sufficient contrast between signer, clothing and background.
- Avoid overlays on the signing space.
- Do not make the sign-language window too small to read.
- Let users enlarge, reposition or focus the signing video.

## Consent and rights

Before publishing, record:

- signer identity or approved public credit;
- the specific consent record;
- permitted uses;
- licence or rights statement;
- retention or removal process;
- whether machine-learning use is allowed. It must not be assumed.

The metadata validator helps identify missing records but does not replace legal review.

## Quality review

Important content should be reviewed by people fluent in the sign language and familiar with the target subject. Do not treat automatic generation or word-for-word signed output as equivalent to a human translation.
